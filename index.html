Scrum in one breath

Founded on

Empiricism and lean thinking

3 pillars

Transparency, inspection, adaptation

5 values

Commitment, focus, openness, respect, courage

Team

10 or fewer people. Cross-functional, self-managing, no sub-teams or titles.

Three accountabilities

WhoAccountable forRemember
Product OwnerMaximizing the value of the product. Product Backlog management: the Product Goal, creating and communicating items, ordering, keeping the backlog transparent.One person, not a committee. Can delegate work but stays accountable. Stakeholders who want changes must convince the PO.
DevelopersThe Sprint Backlog (the plan), quality through the Definition of Done, adapting the plan daily, holding each other accountable.Size Product Backlog items. Own the Sprint Backlog. Select items for the Sprint in discussion with the PO.
Scrum MasterThe Scrum Team's effectiveness and establishing Scrum as defined in the guide.Serves the team, the PO and the organization. Causes impediments to be removed. A true leader who serves.

Five events

EventTimebox (1-month Sprint)WhoPurpose
Sprint≤ 1 monthScrum TeamContainer for all other events. Next Sprint starts right after. Only the PO can cancel, if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.
Sprint Planning≤ 8 hScrum Team (+ invited advisers)Why is this Sprint valuable? What can be Done? How will it get done? Produces the Sprint Goal.
Daily Scrum15 minDevelopersInspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog. Same time and place every day.
Sprint Review≤ 4 hScrum Team + key stakeholdersInspect the outcome and decide next adaptations. A working session, not a demo or a release gate.
Sprint Retrospective≤ 3 hScrum TeamPlan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. Concludes the Sprint.

Shorter Sprints usually have shorter timeboxes.

Artifacts and their commitments

ArtifactCommitmentOwnerKey rules
Product BacklogProduct GoalProduct OwnerEmergent, ordered, single source of work. One per product. Fulfill or abandon a Product Goal before the next.
Sprint BacklogSprint GoalDevelopersSprint Goal (why) + selected items (what) + plan (how). Updated throughout the Sprint. Scope renegotiated with the PO without endangering the Sprint Goal.
IncrementDefinition of DoneScrum TeamUsable, additive, verified. Several per Sprint are allowed. Can be released before Sprint Review. Undone work goes back to the Product Backlog.

EBM: four Key Value Areas

AreaAsksExample measure
Current ValueWhat value do we deliver today?Customer satisfaction
Unrealized ValueHow much more value could we deliver?Market share gap
Time to MarketHow fast can we deliver new value?Release frequency, lead time
Ability to InnovateHow effectively can we deliver new capabilities?Technical debt, time spent on new work

EBM goals: Strategic Goal → Intermediate Goals → Immediate Tactical Goals.

Common traps

  • User stories, story points, velocity, burn-downs and a "Definition of Ready" are not part of Scrum.
  • Velocity measures output, not value. Value is measured by outcomes.
  • The PO doesn't assign tasks, and can't add work to the Sprint Backlog alone.
  • The PO and Scrum Master aren't required at the Daily Scrum.
  • Many teams on one product: one Product Backlog, one PO, one shared Definition of Done.
  • Technical work belongs in the Product Backlog, not a separate backlog.
  • The Sprint Goal doesn't change during the Sprint. The plan to reach it can.
  • Quality doesn't decrease to meet a date.
